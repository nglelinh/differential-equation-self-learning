15-01 Wave Front Set

This illustration highlights the main refinement beyond singular support: a singularity has not only a location, but also preferred directions in frequency space.

Wave Front Set: Position and Direction

Teaching objective. Distinguish a jump edge, a point source, and a corner by their directional singular structure.
Mathematical idea. Local Fourier energy concentrates in normal or otherwise distinguished directions, giving the intuition behind the wave front set.
Interactions. Choose the singularity type, rotate the edge, toggle singular directions, and rotate a probe ray.
Open standalone

15-02 Propagation of Singularities

This demo emphasizes the geometric intuition behind propagation: singularities travel along rays rather than diffusing arbitrarily.

Propagation of Singularities Along Rays

Teaching objective. Show that singularities move along ray families and may reflect from boundaries.
Mathematical idea. In a constant-speed model, the wave front expands with radius ct, representing the simplest bicharacteristic geometry.
Interactions. Drag the source point, adjust time, adjust wave speed, toggle a reflecting wall, and play the animation.
Open standalone

15-04 Semiclassical Analysis

The semiclassical picture becomes much clearer when students can see a packet concentrate as h becomes small and its center track a classical trajectory.

Semiclassical Limit: Wave Packet and Classical Orbit

Teaching objective. Make the correspondence between high-frequency quantum dynamics and classical Hamiltonian motion visible.
Mathematical idea. In a harmonic potential, the packet center follows the classical trajectory while the packet width shrinks as h decreases.
Interactions. Adjust h, the initial position, the initial momentum, and the time evolution.
Open standalone

15-05 Spectral Asymptotics

Students can compare the exact eigenvalue counting function to the Weyl asymptotic law while changing dimension and domain size to see the geometric dependence directly.

Spectral Asymptotics and Weyl's Law

Teaching objective. Show Weyl's law as a geometric growth principle rather than a purely formal asymptotic statement.
Mathematical idea. The counting function N(lambda) grows according to dimension and volume, and approaches the Weyl asymptotic law at high energy.
Interactions. Switch between 1D and 2D, vary domain size, and change lambda max.
Open standalone
