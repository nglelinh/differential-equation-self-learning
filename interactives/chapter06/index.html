Chapter 06 Interactives

This standalone gallery proposes two runnable JavaScript illustrations for each topic in Chapter 06, Series Solutions and Special Functions. The emphasis is on recurrence relations, truncation behavior, singular-point structure, and the geometry of classical special functions such as Bessel and Legendre families.

06-01 Power Series 06-02 Ordinary Points 06-03 Euler 06-04 Frobenius 06-05 Bessel 06-06 Legendre 06-07 Other Special 06-08 Physics

06-01 Power Series Review

These illustrations focus on convergence, truncation, and how finite partial sums build analytic functions locally.

Partial Sums of a Power Series

Teaching objective. Show how higher partial sums improve approximation near the expansion point.
Mathematical idea shown. A power series behaves like an infinite polynomial whose partial sums capture local structure progressively.
User interactions. Dropdown for function and slider for truncation order.

Radius of Convergence Explorer

Teaching objective. Show that convergence depends on distance from the expansion center and coefficient growth.
Mathematical idea shown. The ratio test translates coefficient behavior into a radius of convergence.
User interactions. Slider for the effective radius and movable test point.

06-02 Series Solutions Near Ordinary Points

These illustrations show how substitution into an ODE produces recurrences and how those recurrences generate solution shapes.

Recurrence Coefficients from a Series Ansatz

Teaching objective. Show how the ODE turns the unknown coefficients into a recurrence rule.
Mathematical idea shown. At an ordinary point, a power-series substitution gives algebraic conditions on coefficients.
User interactions. Slider for the number of computed coefficients.

Series Solution Approximation

Teaching objective. Show how the recurrence-generated coefficients build an approximate solution curve.
Mathematical idea shown. The local solution is determined term-by-term once initial coefficients are fixed.
User interactions. Reuses the truncation slider.

06-03 Euler Equations

These illustrations emphasize scale invariance and the special role of power-law trial solutions.

Indicial Roots and Power-Law Modes

Teaching objective. Show how the indicial equation selects admissible power-law behaviors.
Mathematical idea shown. Euler equations reduce to algebra in the exponent $$r$$ when we try $$y=x^r$$.
User interactions. Sliders for the Euler coefficients $$a$$ and $$b$$.

Scale-Invariant Solution Shapes

Teaching objective. Show the contrast between distinct-root, repeated-root, and complex-root Euler behavior.
Mathematical idea shown. The same indicial structure generates powers, log-corrections, or oscillations in $$\ln x$$.
User interactions. Reuses the Euler coefficient sliders.

06-04 The Frobenius Method

These illustrations show why the extra factor $$x^r$$ is necessary at a regular singular point.

Regular Singular Point Detector

Teaching objective. Show how multiplying coefficients by powers of $$x$$ reveals whether a singularity is regular.
Mathematical idea shown. Regular singular points are weak enough for a Frobenius series to work.
User interactions. Dropdown for model coefficient type.

Effect of the Leading Power x^r

Teaching objective. Show how changing the indicial root changes the leading behavior near the singular point.
Mathematical idea shown. The Frobenius exponent sets the dominant local asymptotics before the series corrections appear.
User interactions. Slider for the indicial exponent $$r$$.

06-05 Bessel's Equation

These illustrations focus on oscillation, zeros, and the radial geometry behind Bessel functions.

Bessel-Like Oscillations and Zeros

Teaching objective. Show how Bessel-type oscillations differ from sine waves and why zeros matter in applications.
Mathematical idea shown. Solutions for cylindrical symmetry have nonuniform oscillation spacing and amplitude behavior.
User interactions. Slider for order $$\nu$$.

Radial Mode on a Disk

Teaching objective. Connect zeros of Bessel functions to nodal rings in circular domains.
Mathematical idea shown. Boundary conditions on a disk quantize radial modes through Bessel zeros.
User interactions. Dropdown for radial mode number.

06-06 Legendre's Equation

These illustrations emphasize polynomial structure, oscillation count, and orthogonality on $$[-1,1]$$.

Legendre Polynomial Family

Teaching objective. Show how degree controls oscillation count and shape on the interval $$[-1,1]$$.
Mathematical idea shown. Legendre polynomials form a structured family adapted to spherical symmetry.
User interactions. Slider for degree $$\ell$$.

Orthogonality Demonstration

Teaching objective. Show visually why distinct Legendre polynomials cancel under the orthogonality integral.
Mathematical idea shown. Orthogonality underlies expansion of functions in Legendre bases.
User interactions. Sliders for two polynomial degrees.

06-07 Other Special Functions

These illustrations treat the chapter’s survey functions as a map of families rather than an isolated list.

Hermite, Laguerre, and Chebyshev Snapshot

Teaching objective. Show the distinct natural domains and oscillation patterns of several classical polynomial families.
Mathematical idea shown. Different special functions are adapted to different weights, domains, and physical symmetries.
User interactions. Dropdown for family and slider for degree.

Orthogonality Weight Comparison

Teaching objective. Show that orthogonality depends on the correct weight and interval, not only on the polynomial shape.
Mathematical idea shown. Each family carries its own natural weighted inner product.
User interactions. Reuses the family selector.

06-08 Applications in Physics

These illustrations gather the physical intuition behind why special functions appear in separation-of-variables problems.

Circular Membrane Mode Profile

Teaching objective. Show how Bessel-type radial behavior translates into nodal structure on a drumhead.
Mathematical idea shown. Special functions are mode shapes, not just symbolic solutions.
User interactions. Dropdown for mode family.

Quantized Modes from Boundary Conditions

Teaching objective. Show that boundary conditions select only discrete allowed mode indices or zeros.
Mathematical idea shown. Physical systems often convert continuous equations into discrete spectra.
User interactions. Reuses the mode selector.
