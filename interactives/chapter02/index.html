Chapter 02 Interactives

This standalone gallery proposes two runnable JavaScript illustrations for each topic in Chapter 02, Higher-Order Linear ODEs. The emphasis shifts from local direction fields to mode decomposition, characteristic roots, oscillation regimes, forcing, and basis construction.

02-01 Second-Order Linear Equations: Overview

These opening illustrations emphasize that second-order models need two initial conditions and that the total response splits into natural and forced parts.

Two Initial Conditions, One Trajectory

Teaching objective. Show why position and velocity are both needed to select one solution of a second-order equation.
Mathematical idea shown. For $$y''+\omega^2y=0$$, the two-dimensional homogeneous solution space is fixed by $$y(0)$$ and $$y'(0)$$.
User interactions. Sliders for angular frequency, initial displacement, and initial velocity.

Natural Response Plus Forcing

Teaching objective. Make the homogeneous and particular parts visible in the full solution of a nonhomogeneous equation.
Mathematical idea shown. For $$y''+2\zeta\omega y'+\omega^2 y = A$$, the total response is transient plus equilibrium offset.
User interactions. Sliders for damping ratio, frequency, forcing level, and initial displacement.

02-02 Constant Coefficients: Distinct Real Roots

These illustrations focus on exponential mode mixing and the special structure of repeated roots.

Distinct Real Mode Mixer

Teaching objective. Show how two exponential modes combine and how one mode eventually dominates.
Mathematical idea shown. For roots $$r_1 \neq r_2$$, the solution is $$c_1e^{r_1t}+c_2e^{r_2t}$$.
User interactions. Sliders for roots and coefficients.

Repeated Root Builder

Teaching objective. Explain why a repeated root needs a second basis function $$te^{rt}$$ instead of another copy of $$e^{rt}$$.
Mathematical idea shown. When the characteristic root has multiplicity two, the solution basis becomes $$e^{rt}$$ and $$te^{rt}$$.
User interactions. Sliders for repeated root and the two coefficients.

02-03 Constant Coefficients: Complex Roots

The main visual shift here is from exponentials to oscillatory envelopes and root geometry.

Damped Oscillation Envelope

Teaching objective. Separate the roles of the real and imaginary parts of the characteristic roots.
Mathematical idea shown. With roots $$\alpha \pm i\beta$$, the solution has envelope $$e^{\alpha t}$$ and oscillation rate $$\beta$$.
User interactions. Sliders for $$\alpha$$, $$\beta$$, and the basis coefficients.

Characteristic Roots in the Complex Plane

Teaching objective. Connect equation coefficients to the location of roots and the corresponding time-domain regime.
Mathematical idea shown. For $$y'' + by' + cy = 0$$, the discriminant determines whether roots are real, repeated, or complex.
User interactions. Sliders for coefficients $$b$$ and $$c$$.

02-04 Reduction of Order

These visuals emphasize basis completion and linearly independent structure rather than raw algebraic manipulation.

Known Solution and New Independent Solution

Teaching objective. Show that reduction of order turns one known solution into a full basis.
Mathematical idea shown. For the Cauchy-Euler equation with repeated root 1, one solution is $$y_1=x$$ and the second is $$y_2=x\ln x$$.
User interactions. Sliders for basis coefficients.

Why a Constant Multiple Is Not Enough

Teaching objective. Contrast a genuine second solution with a mere rescaling of the known one.
Mathematical idea shown. The ansatz $$y_2=v(x)y_1(x)$$ must use a nonconstant factor to create independence.
User interactions. Slider for the constant multiple and a slider for the nonconstant factor scale.

02-05 Nonhomogeneous Equations: Undetermined Coefficients

Here the key visual idea is choosing a trial family that stays closed under differentiation and noticing when resonance forces an extra factor of time.

Forcing Family Explorer

Teaching objective. Help students match forcing functions to the right trial family.
Mathematical idea shown. Polynomial, exponential, and sine-cosine forcing remain in manageable function families after differentiation.
User interactions. Dropdown for forcing type and slider for forcing strength.

Resonance Multiplier Demo

Teaching objective. Show why the naive particular guess fails when it overlaps with a homogeneous mode.
Mathematical idea shown. For $$y''-2y'+y=e^t$$, the root $$r=1$$ repeats in the homogeneous solution, so the particular trial must be multiplied by a power of $$t$$.
User interactions. Checkbox to toggle resonance and slider for forcing scale.

02-06 Variation of Parameters

These illustrations show how forcing changes the time-dependent weights of a homogeneous basis.

Changing Basis Weights u1 and u2

Teaching objective. Let students see the new coefficient functions evolve under forcing.
Mathematical idea shown. For $$y''+y=g(t)$$ with basis $$\cos t,\sin t$$, variation of parameters produces time-dependent coefficients $$u_1,u_2$$.
User interactions. Slider for forcing decay rate.

Particular Solution Built from the Basis

Teaching objective. Show that the particular solution is assembled from the same homogeneous directions.
Mathematical idea shown. The forcing steers the coefficients, and their mixture reconstructs $$y_p(t)$$.
User interactions. Slider for forcing decay rate.

02-07 Higher-Order Equations

These demos generalize the second-order picture to three modes and to asymptotic dominance.

Third-Order Mode Mixer

Teaching objective. Extend characteristic-root thinking from two modes to three.
Mathematical idea shown. A third-order constant-coefficient equation can combine three exponential modes.
User interactions. Sliders for three roots and three coefficients.

Dominant Mode Over Time

Teaching objective. Show why long-term behavior is usually governed by the least negative or most positive root.
Mathematical idea shown. The largest real part eventually dominates asymptotic growth or decay.
User interactions. Reuses the same root sliders as the mode mixer.

02-08 Mechanical Vibrations

The central application chapter is about reading damping regimes and resonance directly from the parameters.

Damping Regime Explorer

Teaching objective. Compare overdamped, critically damped, and underdamped motion on one parameter family.
Mathematical idea shown. The discriminant $$c^2-4mk$$ governs the oscillation regime of $$mx''+cx'+kx=0$$.
User interactions. Sliders for mass, damping, spring constant, initial displacement, and initial velocity.

Steady-State Resonance Curve

Teaching objective. Show how forcing frequency controls the steady-state amplitude of a damped oscillator.
Mathematical idea shown. The frequency response curve peaks near the natural frequency when damping is small.
User interactions. Sliders for mass, damping, spring constant, and forcing amplitude.

02-09 RLC Circuits

These illustrations parallel the mechanical vibration models, now in electrical language.

Free RLC Response

Teaching objective. Show the circuit analogy to damped oscillation in charge space.
Mathematical idea shown. The free equation $$Lq''+Rq'+\frac{1}{C}q=0$$ has the same regime structure as a damped spring.
User interactions. Sliders for inductance, resistance, capacitance, initial charge, and initial current.

RLC Frequency Response

Teaching objective. Visualize resonance in circuit current amplitude under sinusoidal forcing.
Mathematical idea shown. The steady-state current amplitude depends on impedance and peaks near the resonant frequency.
User interactions. Sliders for inductance, resistance, capacitance, and source amplitude.

02-10 Wronskian and Linear Independence

The chapter ends by turning “independent solutions” into something students can compute and see.

Function Pair and Wronskian

Teaching objective. Let students compare dependent and independent function pairs.
Mathematical idea shown. The Wronskian detects whether two candidate solutions form a basis locally.
User interactions. Dropdown for function pair.

Abel Formula Explorer

Teaching objective. Show how the coefficient of $$y'$$ controls the evolution of the Wronskian.
Mathematical idea shown. For $$y''+p(t)y'+q(t)y=0$$, Abel's formula gives $$W(t)=Ce^{-\int p(t)dt}$$.
User interactions. Sliders for constant $$p$$ and the integration constant $$C$$.
