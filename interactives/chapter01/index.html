Chapter 01 Interactives

This standalone gallery proposes two JavaScript-based illustrations for each topic in Chapter 01, First-Order Ordinary Differential Equations. Each card includes the teaching brief requested for the lesson and a runnable visualization built with Plotly.js or plain HTML canvas.

01-01 Introduction to Differential Equations

These two opening illustrations focus on the core idea that an ODE prescribes a local law of change and that numerical methods approximate the resulting global curve.

Slope Field and Initial Value Picker

Teaching objective. Connect the symbolic equation $$y' = f(t,y)$$ to a visual field of local directions and a single solution selected by an initial condition.
Mathematical idea shown. A differential equation gives slope information point-by-point, and a chosen point $$\left(t_0,y_0\right)$$ generates a solution curve.
User interactions. Sliders for growth rate, forcing, and initial value.

Exact Solution vs Euler Approximation

Teaching objective. Show that a numerical method follows the same local slope logic but introduces step-size dependent error.
Mathematical idea shown. For $$y' = \lambda y$$, the exact exponential curve and the Euler polygon agree locally but diverge as the step size grows.
User interactions. Sliders for $$\lambda$$, step size, and initial value.

01-02 Separable Equations

The chapter’s first major solution method benefits from visuals that separate model behavior by parameter and by initial condition.

Exponential vs Logistic Growth

Teaching objective. Contrast unrestricted growth with saturation under the logistic model.
Mathematical idea shown. Both models are separable, but the logistic factor $$1-P/K$$ creates an attracting carrying capacity.
User interactions. Dropdown for model type, sliders for rate, carrying capacity, and initial population.

Separable Direction Field

Teaching objective. Make the product form $$y' = g(t)h(y)$$ visible so students see how time and state influence the slope independently.
Mathematical idea shown. The sign pattern comes from factors in time and state, while equilibria appear where $$h(y)=0$$.
User interactions. Sliders for amplitude, carrying capacity, and initial value.

01-03 Linear First-Order Equations

These illustrations emphasize transient versus steady behavior and the effect of forcing on a stable linear system.

Transient and Steady-State Decomposition

Teaching objective. Help students read a linear first-order solution as the sum of a decaying transient and a long-time response.
Mathematical idea shown. For $$y' + py = q$$ with constants, the equilibrium is $$q/p$$ and the transient decays like $$e^{-pt}$$ when $$p>0$$.
User interactions. Sliders for $$p$$, $$q$$, and initial value, plus a checkbox to show the equilibrium line.

Sinusoidal Forcing Response

Teaching objective. Show that a first-order linear equation smooths and delays an oscillatory input rather than copying it exactly.
Mathematical idea shown. The equation $$y' + py = A\sin(\omega t)$$ produces a lagging response whose amplitude depends on the relative size of $$p$$ and $$\omega$$.
User interactions. Sliders for damping, forcing amplitude, forcing frequency, and initial value.

01-04 Exact Equations

Exactness becomes much clearer when students can see solution curves as level sets of a potential function.

Potential Function Level Curves

Teaching objective. Show that solving an exact equation is equivalent to finding a potential whose contours are the solution family.
Mathematical idea shown. When $$dF = F_x dx + F_y dy = 0$$, every solution lies on a contour $$F(x,y)=C$$.
User interactions. Sliders for cross-coupling and the chosen level constant.

Exact vs Non-Exact Field Comparison

Teaching objective. Contrast a gradient-type field that comes from a potential with a swirling field that does not.
Mathematical idea shown. Exactness is tied to path-independent structure and zero curl in this planar analogy.
User interactions. Dropdown for field type and a slider for field intensity.

01-05 Substitution Methods

Substitution is easier to teach when students can see the original equation and the transformed variable changing together.

Bernoulli Equation Transformation

Teaching objective. Show how a nonlinear Bernoulli equation becomes linear after the power substitution.
Mathematical idea shown. For $$y' + py = qy^n$$, the substitution $$u = y^{1-n}$$ converts the equation into a first-order linear equation in $$u$$.
User interactions. Sliders for $$p$$, $$q$$, exponent $$n$$, and initial value.

Homogeneous Equation and Ratio Variable

Teaching objective. Help students see why the ratio $$v=y/x$$ is the natural variable for a first-order homogeneous equation.
Mathematical idea shown. If $$y' = F(y/x)$$, then the direction depends only on the ray from the origin, not on absolute position.
User interactions. Sliders for parameter c and the starting point on the line $$x=1$$.

01-06 Existence and Uniqueness

These visuals are designed to make the abstract language of local existence and Lipschitz continuity feel concrete.

Unique and Non-Unique Initial Value Problems

Teaching objective. Show the memorable contrast between a well-posed IVP and one with multiple solutions through the same point.
Mathematical idea shown. The model $$y' = y^{1/3}, y(0)=0$$ admits many solutions, unlike smooth Lipschitz examples such as $$y'=y$$.
User interactions. Dropdown for scenario and a slider that changes the waiting time before a non-unique branch departs from zero.

Lipschitz Threshold Explorer

Teaching objective. Let students experiment with a family of right-hand sides and see when uniqueness at the origin becomes fragile.
Mathematical idea shown. For $$f(y)=|y|^\alpha$$, the graph is continuous for every $$\alpha>0$$, but local Lipschitz behavior near zero changes at $$\alpha=1$$.
User interactions. Slider for exponent $$\alpha$$.

01-07 Applications: Population and Mixing

These applications turn the formal solution methods into interpretable models with equilibrium and balance-law language.

Logistic Growth with Harvesting

Teaching objective. Show how a small model modification can change long-term population behavior dramatically.
Mathematical idea shown. The equation $$P' = rP(1-P/K)-H$$ adds a constant harvest term that can shift or remove equilibria.
User interactions. Sliders for growth rate, carrying capacity, harvest level, and initial population.

Mixing Tank Balance Law

Teaching objective. Reinforce the phrase “rate in minus rate out” with a visual tank model and a concentration graph.
Mathematical idea shown. For a constant-volume tank, the amount of dissolved salt satisfies a first-order linear ODE with an equilibrium concentration.
User interactions. Sliders for inflow concentration, flow rate, tank volume, initial concentration, and a play or pause button.

01-08 Applications: Mechanics and Circuits

These illustrations link the first-order linear framework to physical parameters such as drag, terminal velocity, and time constants.

Falling Body with Linear Drag

Teaching objective. Show how drag creates a stable terminal velocity rather than indefinite acceleration.
Mathematical idea shown. The velocity equation $$mv' = mg-cv$$ is linear and approaches the equilibrium velocity $$v_T = mg/c$$.
User interactions. Sliders for mass, drag coefficient, gravity, and a play or pause button.

RC Step Response

Teaching objective. Tie the general first-order linear solution to the familiar charging curve of a capacitor.
Mathematical idea shown. The equation $$RCV' + V = V_{in}$$ has the characteristic time scale $$\tau = RC$$ and approaches the source voltage asymptotically.
User interactions. Sliders for resistance, capacitance, source voltage, initial capacitor voltage, and a play or pause button.

01-09 Autonomous Equations and Phase Lines

These optional chapter-ending interactives push students toward qualitative reasoning without requiring explicit solutions.

Phase Line and Time Trace

Teaching objective. Connect the sign of $$f(y)$$ on the phase line to the time evolution of a solution.
Mathematical idea shown. Equilibria partition the phase line, and arrows determined by the sign of $$f(y)$$ predict long-term motion.
User interactions. Sliders for equilibrium locations, growth rate, and initial value.

Parameter Sensitivity and Stability Change

Teaching objective. Show how a parameter can create or destroy equilibria in an autonomous ODE.
Mathematical idea shown. For $$y' = \mu - y^2$$, the number and stability of equilibria depend directly on the sign of $$\mu$$.
User interactions. Slider for parameter $$\mu$$.
