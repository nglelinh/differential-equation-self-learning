Chapter 05 Interactives

This standalone gallery proposes two runnable JavaScript illustrations for each topic in Chapter 05, Nonlinear Dynamics and Stability. The emphasis is geometric: nullclines, trajectories, local linearization, Lyapunov landscapes, limit cycles, bifurcation diagrams, ecological models, chaos sensitivity, and SIR thresholds.

05-01 Phase Plane 05-02 Linearization 05-03 Stability 05-04 Lyapunov 05-05 Limit Cycles 05-06 Bifurcations 05-07 Predator-Prey 05-08 Competition 05-09 Chaos 05-10 SIR

05-01 Autonomous Systems and the Phase Plane

These opening illustrations shift the focus from explicit formulas to geometry of flow, nullclines, and regions of motion.

Nonlinear Vector Field and Nullclines

Teaching objective. Show how nullclines divide the phase plane into regions with different motion directions.
Mathematical idea shown. For a planar autonomous system, local arrows depend only on state, not explicitly on time.
User interactions. Sliders for the nonlinear parameters.

Trajectories from Different Initial Conditions

Teaching objective. Show that the same nonlinear field can guide nearby initial conditions into very different regions.
Mathematical idea shown. Phase-plane structure organizes families of trajectories even when no explicit formula is available.
User interactions. Sliders for two initial coordinates.

05-02 Equilibria and Linearization

These illustrations show how a nonlinear field becomes approximately linear under magnification near an equilibrium.

Zooming Near an Equilibrium

Teaching objective. Show that local nonlinear geometry becomes nearly linear close to an equilibrium.
Mathematical idea shown. The Jacobian gives the first-order approximation to the vector field near the steady state.
User interactions. Slider for zoom scale.

Nonlinear vs Linearized Trajectory

Teaching objective. Compare the true nonlinear trajectory to the trajectory predicted by the Jacobian.
Mathematical idea shown. Linearization is accurate locally but can drift away from the true orbit farther out.
User interactions. Sliders for initial perturbation size.

05-03 Stability Classification

These illustrations connect Jacobian spectra to local geometry while keeping the nonlinear caveat in view.

Jacobian Spectrum Classifier

Teaching objective. Show how eigenvalues of the Jacobian determine local node, saddle, spiral, or center-type behavior.
Mathematical idea shown. Real parts control attraction or repulsion, while imaginary parts create rotation.
User interactions. Sliders for real part and imaginary part.

Representative Local Phase Portrait

Teaching objective. Show the geometric motion corresponding to the chosen spectral type.
Mathematical idea shown. Local classification translates eigenvalue information into orbit shapes.
User interactions. Reuses the spectral sliders.

05-04 Lyapunov Stability

These visuals make Lyapunov functions feel like geometric energy landscapes rather than only symbolic inequalities.

Lyapunov Landscape and Descent

Teaching objective. Show how a scalar function decreases along trajectories without solving the system explicitly.
Mathematical idea shown. A positive function with nonpositive derivative along trajectories acts as a stability certificate.
User interactions. Sliders for damping and initial state.

V(t) Along the Trajectory

Teaching objective. Show the monotone decay of a Lyapunov candidate along a solution.
Mathematical idea shown. If $$\dot V < 0$$ away from equilibrium, the system is driven toward lower-energy states.
User interactions. Reuses the damping and initial-condition sliders.

05-05 Limit Cycles

These illustrations distinguish isolated periodic orbits from ordinary families of closed curves.

Stable Limit Cycle in a Radial Normal Form

Teaching objective. Show a phase portrait where nearby trajectories are drawn to an isolated periodic orbit.
Mathematical idea shown. In polar form, an attracting radius can create a stable limit cycle.
User interactions. Slider for target cycle radius.

Closed Orbit Family vs Isolated Cycle

Teaching objective. Contrast a nonlinear limit cycle with a linear center that has many closed orbits.
Mathematical idea shown. Isolation, not merely periodicity, is what defines a limit cycle.
User interactions. Dropdown between center and isolated-cycle model.

05-06 Bifurcations

These illustrations make parameter thresholds visible as branch creation, destruction, and stability exchange.

One-Parameter Normal Form Explorer

Teaching objective. Let students move the parameter through a local bifurcation and watch equilibrium branches change.
Mathematical idea shown. Saddle-node, transcritical, and pitchfork normal forms show different regime changes.
User interactions. Dropdown for bifurcation type and slider for parameter $$\mu$$.

Vector Field Before and After Threshold

Teaching objective. Show that a bifurcation is a structural change in the underlying dynamics, not just a graph decoration.
Mathematical idea shown. The sign pattern of the one-dimensional flow changes as equilibria are created or lose stability.
User interactions. Reuses the type and parameter controls.

05-07 Predator-Prey Models

These illustrations focus on nullclines, positive equilibrium, and cyclic interaction.

Lotka-Volterra Phase Portrait

Teaching objective. Show how the interaction geometry produces cyclical predator-prey motion.
Mathematical idea shown. Nullclines and the positive equilibrium organize the cycle in the positive quadrant.
User interactions. Sliders for prey growth and predator death rates.

Predator and Prey Time Traces

Teaching objective. Show the time lag between prey peaks and predator peaks.
Mathematical idea shown. Coupled nonlinear feedback creates oscillations with a biologically meaningful phase lag.
User interactions. Reuses the parameter sliders.

05-08 Competing Species

These illustrations emphasize nullcline geometry and the balance between coexistence and exclusion.

Competition Nullclines and Flow

Teaching objective. Show how nullcline placement predicts coexistence or exclusion outcomes.
Mathematical idea shown. The competition coefficients determine whether the interior equilibrium exists and is attracting.
User interactions. Sliders for cross-competition coefficients $$\alpha$$ and $$\beta$$.

Outcome Regions

Teaching objective. Show how the same model can lead to coexistence, bistability, or exclusion depending on parameters.
Mathematical idea shown. Competitive outcomes are encoded geometrically before solving trajectories in detail.
User interactions. Reuses the competition sliders.

05-09 Introduction to Chaos

These illustrations are introductory by design: sensitivity, boundedness, and the distinction between determinism and predictability.

Sensitive Dependence Toy Model

Teaching objective. Show how tiny initial differences can grow dramatically even under deterministic rules.
Mathematical idea shown. Positive effective Lyapunov growth causes nearby trajectories to separate exponentially for a time.
User interactions. Slider for initial separation and slider for growth exponent.

Logistic Map Bifurcation Preview

Teaching objective. Give an accessible first look at how deterministic iteration can create complex long-run behavior.
Mathematical idea shown. Period-doubling and irregular attractor-like structure appear as the parameter increases.
User interactions. Slider for the logistic map parameter.

05-10 Applications: SIR Epidemiology

These illustrations center the threshold idea $$\mathcal{R}_0$$ and the flow among compartments.

SIR Compartment Trajectories

Teaching objective. Show the standard epidemic curve: susceptible down, infected up then down, removed up.
Mathematical idea shown. Transmission and recovery rates determine whether infections initially grow and when they peak.
User interactions. Sliders for transmission $$\beta$$ and recovery $$\gamma$$.

Threshold and Effective Growth

Teaching objective. Show how $$\mathcal{R}_0$$ and the current susceptible pool determine whether infections rise or fall.
Mathematical idea shown. Growth depends on the sign of $$\dot I = I(\beta S - \gamma)$$.
User interactions. Reuses the transmission and recovery sliders.
