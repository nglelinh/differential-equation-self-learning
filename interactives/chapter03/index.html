Chapter 03 Interactives

This standalone gallery proposes two runnable JavaScript illustrations for each topic in Chapter 03, The Laplace Transform. The emphasis is on how time-domain signals are encoded into the Laplace domain, how inverse transforms recover familiar behavior, and how step, impulse, convolution, and transfer-function ideas fit together.

03-01 Definition and Basic Properties

These opening illustrations make the transform feel like a weighted view of signal history rather than a table lookup.

Exponential Weighting Lens

Teaching objective. Show how the factor $$e^{-st}$$ changes which part of the signal matters in the integral.
Mathematical idea shown. The Laplace transform is the weighted area $$\int_0^\infty e^{-st}f(t)\,dt$$, with larger $$s$$ suppressing the tail more strongly.
User interactions. Dropdown for signal type and slider for $$s$$.

Linearity in the Laplace Domain

Teaching objective. Show that combining time-domain signals combines their transforms with the same coefficients.
Mathematical idea shown. $$\mathcal{L}\{af+bg\}=aF+bG$$ is a structural property, not just a table shortcut.
User interactions. Sliders for coefficients $$a$$ and $$b$$.

03-02 Laplace Transform of Elementary Functions

These illustrations are about building table intuition from patterns, poles, and simple families.

Elementary Function Table Builder

Teaching objective. Connect a familiar time-domain function to its Laplace-domain graph.
Mathematical idea shown. Constants, polynomials, exponentials, and trig functions map to simple rational expressions in $$s$$.
User interactions. Dropdown for function family and slider for parameter.

Pole Shift Explorer

Teaching objective. Show how exponential growth or decay shifts the pole location in the Laplace domain.
Mathematical idea shown. $$\mathcal{L}\{e^{at}\}=1/(s-a)$$ moves the singular feature from $$s=0$$ to $$s=a$$.
User interactions. Slider for shift parameter $$a$$.

03-03 Inverse Laplace Transform

These visuals show inverse Laplace as reading time behavior back out of a rational expression in $$s$$.

Partial Fraction to Time Modes

Teaching objective. Help students see partial fractions as mode separation rather than bookkeeping.
Mathematical idea shown. Terms like $$A/(s-a)$$ and $$B/(s-b)$$ decode into exponentials $$Ae^{at}$$ and $$Be^{bt}$$.
User interactions. Sliders for coefficients and pole locations.

Quadratic Pattern Decoder

Teaching objective. Show how quadratic denominators encode oscillatory behavior.
Mathematical idea shown. Expressions of the form $$(Cs+D)/(s^2+\omega^2)$$ invert to combinations of cosine and sine.
User interactions. Sliders for $$C$$, $$D$$, and $$\omega$$.

03-04 Solving Initial Value Problems with Laplace

These demos emphasize the algebraic workflow and the way initial conditions enter immediately in the transform of derivatives.

Derivative Transform with Initial Conditions

Teaching objective. Make the formula $$\mathcal{L}\{y'\}=sY-y(0)$$ and its second-order analogue feel operational.
Mathematical idea shown. Initial data become algebraic correction terms in the Laplace domain.
User interactions. Sliders for $$y(0)$$ and $$y'(0)$$.

Laplace Solution of a Second-Order IVP

Teaching objective. Show the full time response for a classic transform-solvable IVP.
Mathematical idea shown. For $$y''+3y'+2y=0$$, the Laplace-domain solution is algebraic and inverts to a sum of exponentials set by initial data.
User interactions. Reuses sliders for $$y(0)$$ and $$y'(0)$$.

03-05 Step Functions and Discontinuous Forcing

These illustrations show that delayed and piecewise signals can be handled as one algebraic object rather than many separate intervals.

Heaviside Shift and the Factor e^(-as)

Teaching objective. Connect a time delay in the signal to exponential shifting in the Laplace domain.
Mathematical idea shown. $$u(t-a)f(t-a)$$ is a right shift in time and produces the factor $$e^{-as}F(s)$$.
User interactions. Slider for delay time $$a$$.

First-Order Response to a Switched Input

Teaching objective. Show why Laplace is convenient for systems that turn on suddenly.
Mathematical idea shown. The switched input creates a delayed transient in a stable first-order system.
User interactions. Sliders for delay and input height.

03-06 Impulse Functions and the Delta Distribution

These visuals make the limiting pulse intuition and impulse response idea explicit.

Narrow Pulse Approaching Delta

Teaching objective. Show the delta distribution as the limit of pulses with shrinking width and fixed area.
Mathematical idea shown. Height grows as width shrinks so the total area stays constant.
User interactions. Slider for pulse width.

Impulse Response of a Stable First-Order System

Teaching objective. Show that a delta input creates an immediate state jump that then decays according to system dynamics.
Mathematical idea shown. For $$y'+y=\delta(t-a)$$, the response is zero before the impulse and exponential after it.
User interactions. Sliders for impulse time and impulse strength.

03-07 The Convolution Theorem

These illustrations shift the chapter from solving equations to thinking in the language of systems and memory.

Sliding Overlap in Convolution

Teaching objective. Show that convolution measures weighted overlap of past input with shifted response memory.
Mathematical idea shown. $$(f*g)(t)=\int_0^t f(\tau)g(t-\tau)\,d\tau$$ accumulates delayed influence.
User interactions. Slider for observation time $$t$$.

Time Convolution vs Laplace Product

Teaching objective. Show that convolution in time becomes multiplication in the Laplace domain.
Mathematical idea shown. For simple exponentials, the computed convolution matches the inverse transform of the product $$F(s)G(s)$$.
User interactions. Sliders for decay rates of $$f$$ and $$g$$.

03-08 Transfer Functions and Systems

These final illustrations connect the Laplace chapter to filtering, poles, and system behavior.

Low-Pass Transfer Function Magnitude

Teaching objective. Show how a transfer function acts as a frequency-dependent filter.
Mathematical idea shown. For $$H(s)=1/(\tau s+1)$$, high frequencies are attenuated more strongly than low frequencies.
User interactions. Slider for time constant $$\tau$$.

Impulse Response and Step Response

Teaching objective. Show that the transfer function, impulse response, and step response are three views of the same system.
Mathematical idea shown. For a first-order stable system, the impulse response decays exponentially and the step response accumulates it.
User interactions. Reuses the time-constant slider.
