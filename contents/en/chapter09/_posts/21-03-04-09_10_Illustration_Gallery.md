## Purpose of This Companion Lesson

This lesson gathers the Chapter 09 illustrations into one visual review space. The heat equation is often the first PDE students study in full detail, and its conceptual load is substantial: physical derivation, eigenfunction expansions, boundary effects, kernel methods, maximum principles, and numerical approximations all appear within a single chapter. The gallery helps those ideas remain connected rather than fragmented.

A chapter like this becomes much easier to retain when students can move repeatedly between analysis and image. A Gaussian heat kernel, for example, is not only a formula but a picture of spreading and smoothing. A maximum principle is not only an inequality proof but a geometric statement about how extremes behave under diffusion.

## How to Use This Lesson

Work through the gallery in the same order as the chapter. After viewing each figure, return to the corresponding lesson and ask which mathematical idea the image is encoding: conservation law, spectral decomposition, boundary-driven mode selection, homogenization, convolution, qualitative control, or numerical stability. This makes the gallery a bridge between symbolic derivations and physical meaning.

The page is also useful as a review before Chapter 10 and Chapter 11, since the heat equation establishes many of the structural habits later reused in wave propagation and elliptic theory.

## Suggested Study Prompts

1. Which figures emphasize the physical origin of the PDE, and which emphasize the analytic structure of its solutions?
2. Where does the chapter move from exact representation formulas to qualitative control?
3. How do bounded-domain and unbounded-domain pictures prepare the transition from Fourier series to Fourier transforms?
